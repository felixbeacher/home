Your decisions echo

Every country pursues its strategy each quarter. Readiness improves autonomously and changes combat strength. Industrial investment builds growth momentum, which decays by 28% each quarter. Crisis choices can permanently alter quarterly growth (capped at ±1 percentage point).

Cyber operations, trade barriers, proxy conflicts and propaganda carry explicit 2–3 quarter blowback risks. Sanctions and alliance betrayal can cascade into unrest one quarter later. Repair bilateral relations to +20 and end hostilities to avert a pending bilateral event. The Consequences tab lists known risks and their resolution.

The Strategic Roadmap records first-achieved milestones; bars can fall as conditions deteriorate. Only your selected campaign’s victory conditions determine victory. The Nation Intelligence Ledger preserves opening-quarter snapshots plus a separate current position; older saves begin recording when upgraded.

A larger, playable world

15 playable nations. All 14 foreign cabinets respond every quarter. Crises pause resolution and persist across reloads. Every nation starts with allies and rivals. These are fictional 2030 scenario ties, not claims about current defense treaties or hostility. Brazil–China and Nigeria–Russia are invented trade rivalries for gameplay. An enemy is a country with relations at −20 or below, or an active conflict; diplomacy can change this.

Peace victory needs seven pacts, not all fourteen, plus three peaceful quarters. Nuclear capability is a scenario attribute; Iran is non-nuclear in this scenario. New nations join older eight-country saves automatically.

Brinkmanship and troop movements

Forward deployments last three quarters, cost upkeep, and add tension each quarter. Recall forces to remove all your deployments immediately. At 45 tension you can spend a decision on the crisis hotline to lower tension by 12. At 70 the world enters high alert; at 85 it is critical. At 100, nuclear war ends the campaign. Nuclear alert raises tension by 13: inspect the escalation watch before issuing it.

Money and the economy

GDP is annual national output in US$ trillions (tn). Your policy budget and decision costs are in US$ billions (bn). The budget is a discretionary fund for new initiatives, not total government spending or national wealth. Costs scale with your country so every nation stays playable.

Starting GDP values are rounded fictional 2030 scenario assumptions, not forecasts: US $35tn, China $24tn, Germany and India $5.5tn each, UK $4.5tn, Brazil and Russia $2.8tn each, Nigeria $0.4tn. Every nation begins with a policy fund equal to 0.65% of its starting annual GDP. Growth, revenues and policy effects are simplified for gameplay.

Field manual

Your first quarter

  1. Select a nation on the map to inspect its relationships, assets and priorities.
  2. Spend up to two decision points. Every order shows its cost and likely effects before you commit.
  3. End the turn. Foreign leaders act, conflicts evolve, your treasury receives revenue, and a world event may occur.
  4. Respond to offers in the intelligence wire. Offers remain open through the displayed expiry turn. Accepting or declining costs one decision point.
  5. Watch stability and tension. Approval below 15, treasury at or below −20, GDP below 40% of its start, or tension reaching 100 ends the campaign.

Trade gives access to a partner’s resources. Tariffs lower growth while raising revenue; embargos sever trade. Conflicts drain both sides and can pull in defense allies. Peacekeeping, diplomacy and welfare help contain crises.

Goals are measured against your chosen scenario. Each nation can win, but weaker economies face a longer climb. Campaigns last up to 50 turns.
